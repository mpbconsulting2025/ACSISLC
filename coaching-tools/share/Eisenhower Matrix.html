The Eisenhower Matrix

Sort the week before it sorts you. Write every commitment into a box - then act on the box, not the feeling.

Why this may help

Separating urgency from importance can reduce overwhelm and show what to do, schedule, delegate or stop.

Urgent
Not urgent
Important
1
DO
Urgent & important
Handle it today - then ask why it got here.
· What genuinely breaks if this doesn’t happen today?
· Is this urgent because of the task, or because I left it late?
· If I were on leave this week, who would deal with it?
2
SCHEDULE
Important, not urgent
The quadrant that changes your year. Protect it in the diary.
· What will become a crisis next month if I ignore it now?
· When exactly is this booked - and who has been told to respect that slot?
· What here builds the people around me rather than the in-tray?
Not important
3
DELEGATE
Urgent, not important
Someone else’s development opportunity, sitting on your desk.
· Am I holding this because I’m the only one who can - or the fastest?
· Who would grow from owning it, and what would I need to give them?
· What is it actually costing me if they do it at 80%?
4
DECLINE
Neither urgent nor important
Say no once, properly, rather than half-doing it for months.
· What am I avoiding by doing this instead?
· If I stopped entirely, who would notice - and would they be right to?
· Is this a decision I’ve made, or a habit I’ve inherited?

One rule: nothing leaves this page until it has a name against it and a slot in the diary.
