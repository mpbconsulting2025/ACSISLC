Scripts

When you know you need to talk to someone but the first sentence feels difficult, use this page to prepare. It is a starting point, not a speech you have to follow perfectly.

Why this may help

Rehearsing an opening can reduce uncertainty and help you enter a difficult conversation with clarity, respect and a realistic request.

1

Choose the person. Who feels appropriate, available and safe enough to approach?

2

Say enough to begin. You do not need the whole story ready before asking to talk.

3

Make the request clear. Tell them whether you need listening, advice, practical help or a next step.

Starting lines you can borrow
Ask for time: “Could we find ten minutes to talk? There is something I have been finding difficult, and I would value your support.”
When words are hard: “I am not quite sure how to explain this yet, but I think I need to talk to someone about it.”
Say what you need: “I am not asking you to fix everything. What would help most is for you to listen and help me think about the next step.”
Hold a boundary: “I want to talk about this, but I am not ready to discuss every detail. Can we begin with what I need right now?”

Below 5? Make the first step smaller, such as sending a short message to ask for time.

Safety matters. If speaking to this person could put you at risk, or the situation involves immediate danger or a safeguarding concern, use an appropriate emergency, safeguarding or specialist support route instead of relying on this worksheet.

The aim is not perfect wording. It is a clear enough beginning that lets the conversation happen.
