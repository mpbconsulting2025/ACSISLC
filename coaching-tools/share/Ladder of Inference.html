Ladder of Inference

We climb from what we saw to what we did in a fraction of a second, and we rarely notice the rungs. Slowing the climb separates facts from the story we told ourselves about them.

Why this may help

Helps separate facts from the meanings and assumptions added to them, which can open up more accurate choices.

1

Start at the top. What did you do or feel like doing? Write it in rung 7.

2

Climb down, one rung at a time, until you reach the plain facts a camera would have caught.

3

Test the story. At rung 3 and 4, what else could the same facts mean? What would you need to ask to know?

7

based on my beliefs.
6

about the world, which shape what I notice next time.
5

from those assumptions.
4

3

cultural and personal.
2

1

what a video camera would have recorded.

The reflexive loop. Beliefs (rung 6) decide what data we select next time (rung 2). That is how a first impression becomes permanent. Chris Argyris, popularised by Peter Senge in The Fifth Discipline.
