After Action Review (AAR)

Turn an experience into useful learning without blame. Use this after a project, decision, event or difficult moment while the detail is still fresh.

Why this may help

A structured review helps turn experience into learning while protecting what worked and avoiding blame.

Use facts first.
Separate what happened from interpretation.
Keep strengths.
Notice what worked and why it worked.
Finish with action.
Choose what to sustain or change next time.

1. What was supposed to happen?

What was the intended outcome, plan or expectation?

2. What actually happened?

Record observable events and results before judging them.

3. What worked well, and why?

Which choices, behaviours or conditions helped?

4. What could work better, and why?

Look for causes, gaps and conditions, not people to blame.

5. What have I learned?

6. What will I do next time?

An AAR is a learning conversation, not a post-mortem or blame exercise. Adapted for coaching from established after-action review practice.
