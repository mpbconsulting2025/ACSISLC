Coaching framework · Clarity & Connection

FUEL

Use FUEL when a topic needs space and depth before moving to action. The coach holds the structure; the client supplies the meaning, choices and decisions.

Why this may help

Helps explore an issue in depth before rushing to solutions, then turns the desired future into a success plan.

Agree the focus.
Do not assume that the first issue named is the real topic.
Stay curious.
Use open questions, reflections and pauses before offering ideas.
Build ownership.
The success plan should belong to the client, not the worksheet.
F

Frame the conversation

Agree the topic, purpose, boundaries and what would make this conversation useful.

Prompts: What matters most today? What outcome do you want from our time?

U

Understand the current state

Explore facts, perspectives, feelings, patterns, impact and what has already been tried.

Prompts: What is happening now? What is working? What assumptions need testing?

E

Explore the desired state

Describe success and generate more than one path towards it, including barriers and support.

Prompts: What would be different? What options exist? What might get in the way?

L

Lay out a success plan

Turn the chosen route into specific actions, ownership, timing, support and review.

Prompts: What will you do first? By when? Who can help? How will you review progress?

FUEL was developed by John Zenger and Kathleen Stinnett and published in The Extraordinary Coach. See the Zenger Folkman overview.
