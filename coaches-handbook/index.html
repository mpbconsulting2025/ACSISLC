Safeguarding comes before coaching. Stay calm, listen, do not investigate and do not promise secrecy. Where there is immediate danger call 999. In every scenario, notify ACSIS Safeguarding Lead Sam Kinsey-Briggs and complete the report below.

Safeguarding question flow

Choose the concern to see the next steps
What type of concern do you suspect?

Immediate danger

  1. Stop the coaching process and establish the person’s location and immediate safety.
  2. Call 999, or support the client to call. Stay connected where safe and practical.
  3. Share only the information needed for emergency action.
  4. Record the words used, observations, decisions, times and people contacted.
Notify Sam Kinsey-Briggs immediately, then complete and email the safeguarding report to sam@acsis.co.uk.

Suicide or self-harm

  1. Ask directly and calmly: Are you thinking about suicide? Have you made a plan? Do you have the means? When might you act? Can you keep yourself safe right now?
  2. If life is at risk, there is a plan with intent or means, an attempt or overdose, or they cannot stay safe, call 999 or go to A&E.
  3. If urgent but not an emergency, call NHS 111 and select the mental health option, or support them to contact their GP or crisis team.
  4. Do not leave an immediately unsafe person alone. In remote coaching, confirm their location and identify someone nearby who can help.
  5. Record facts and actions, not a clinical judgement.
Notify Sam Kinsey-Briggs in all cases and complete the safeguarding report.

Concern about a child

  1. Listen and reassure. Do not ask leading questions, investigate or confront the alleged person responsible.
  2. Clarify only what is necessary to understand immediate safety: who, what, where and when.
  3. If immediate danger, call 999. Otherwise contact the relevant local authority children’s safeguarding service or police as directed by the Safeguarding Lead.
  4. Do not delay sharing because proof is unavailable. Record the child’s words as accurately as possible.
Notify Sam Kinsey-Briggs promptly, even when the concern seems uncertain or historic.

Concern about an adult at risk

  1. Consider immediate danger, capacity, coercion and whether other people may also be at risk.
  2. Seek the adult’s views and consent where safe and appropriate. A serious risk may justify sharing without consent.
  3. If immediate danger, call 999. Otherwise consult the Safeguarding Lead and the relevant local authority adult safeguarding service.
  4. Record the concern, wishes, consent decision, actions and reasons.
Notify Sam Kinsey-Briggs in every scenario.

Risk to another person

  1. Clarify who may be at risk, the nature and immediacy of the threat, access to weapons or means, and the person’s location.
  2. For a credible immediate threat call 999. Do not warn or confront a potential victim yourself where this could increase risk.
  3. Share relevant information with emergency services and the Safeguarding Lead.
  4. Record exact words, context, times and actions.
Notify Sam Kinsey-Briggs immediately.

Terrorism or radicalisation concern

  1. Do not investigate, challenge ideology or alert anyone who may increase the risk.
  2. If there is immediate danger or an active terrorist-related threat, call 999.
  3. For a non-immediate concern, notify the Safeguarding Lead for consideration of a Prevent referral. The national police Prevent advice line is 0800 011 3764.
  4. Record the specific behaviour or words that caused concern, without labels or assumptions.
Notify Sam Kinsey-Briggs in all terrorism or radicalisation scenarios.

Historic or non-immediate disclosure

  1. Listen without investigating and check whether anyone is currently at risk.
  2. Explain that ACSIS may need to share information to protect the client or another person.
  3. Consult the Safeguarding Lead before deciding no further action is needed.
  4. Record what was disclosed, current risk considerations and the decision made.
Notify Sam Kinsey-Briggs and complete the report.

Unsure, serious welfare concern or outside coaching scope

  1. Pause coaching and clarify what support is needed now.
  2. Check for immediate risk to self, others, a child or an adult at risk.
  3. Signpost or refer to an appropriate clinical, statutory or specialist service.
  4. Seek safeguarding advice rather than holding the uncertainty alone.
Notify Sam Kinsey-Briggs, including when the decision is that no referral is required.
